## Before You Adapt This: Three Rules

**Rule 1: Personalize or lose impact.** Agencies weigh substantive, individualized comments far more heavily than identical form letters. Use this template for structure, but rewrite the opening in your own words and replace the bracketed example with something you actually witnessed. One real story from your unit is worth more than every talking point below.

**Rule 2: Answer what CMS actually asked.** This RFI asks specific questions: whether the current CPT code development process reflects modern clinical practice, whether there are viable alternatives to the CPT and RUC processes, and what alternative governance structures could improve transparency in payment policy. Frame your comment as answers to those questions, not as a general grievance.

**Rule 3: Be constructive.** The strongest comments do not just describe the problem. They tell CMS what evidence exists or could exist. The nursing argument is strongest when it says: the data infrastructure to value nursing work is buildable, and here is what it looks like.

## Comment Template

> **Re: CMS-1848-P — Request for Information on the CPT Coding System and RUC Valuation Process**
>
> Dear Administrator,
>
> I am a [YOUR ROLE: registered nurse / director of nursing informatics / wound care nurse / CNO] with [X] years of experience in [YOUR SETTING: acute inpatient care at a community medical center]. I am writing in response to the Request for Information regarding CMS's reliance on the CPT coding system and the AMA/RUC valuation process. I write as an individual; my views are my own.
>
> I appreciate that CMS is asking whether the current coding and valuation framework reflects modern clinical practice. From where I work, the answer is no. The clearest evidence is the largest segment of the health care workforce, which the framework cannot see at all.
>
> **1. The current framework classifies nursing judgment as overhead**
>
> Registered nurse labor is valued within the Practice Expense component of the RVU, in the same category as supplies and equipment. The Work component, where clinical decision-making, skill, and risk are valued, is attributed to the billing physician. There are no CPT codes that describe RN-delivered services with any specificity. The result is that clinical judgment exercised by nurses at the bedside every day is structurally invisible in the valuation system Medicare uses to determine what health care work is worth.
>
> This is not an abstract concern. It shapes productivity models, staffing budgets, and care delivery decisions at every institution that treats Medicare beneficiaries.
>
> **2. An example from my own practice**
>
> [REPLACE THIS PARAGRAPH WITH YOUR OWN EXAMPLE. A model version follows.]
>
> In inpatient wound care, the nursing assessment is the original source document for events with direct payment consequences. Whether a pressure injury was present on admission is determined by the admitting nurse's assessment. A correctly documented Stage 3 or Stage 4 pressure injury, coded as a secondary diagnosis, is a Major Complication or Comorbidity that changes the DRG assignment. The clinical decisions involved, staging the wound, determining admission status, and selecting treatment based on wound bed condition and patient factors, are made by nurses. Yet none of that decision-making is describable, codeable, or valued anywhere in the CPT/RUC framework. The work that drives the payment outcome has no representation in the payment system.
>
> **3. Response to the RFI questions on alternatives**
>
> As CMS considers whether the survey-based RUC process should be replaced with more objective, auditable data sources, I urge the agency to recognize that the same principle applies to the nursing workforce. Modern electronic health records can passively capture measured assessment time, discrete clinical decisions, and patient complexity data from documentation nurses already complete. This is exactly the kind of empirical, verifiable cost and work data CMS states it prefers over unreliable survey estimates. For nursing, it has never been collected, because no payment framework has ever asked for it.
>
> Any alternative or successor framework to CPT and the RUC should:
>
> - Include a standardized taxonomy capable of describing RN-delivered services and the clinical judgment they involve, rather than folding all nursing labor into an undifferentiated practice expense category.
> - Value services using empirical data captured from clinical systems, including measured time, documented decisions, and patient complexity, rather than specialty society surveys.
> - Include governance representation from nursing, the largest licensed clinical workforce in the country, in any body that succeeds or supplements the RUC.
>
> **4. Why this matters to Medicare**
>
> When nursing work is invisible in valuation, it is minimized in staffing and budgeting. The evidence connecting nurse staffing to patient outcomes, readmissions, and hospital-acquired conditions is extensive. A valuation framework that cannot see nursing cannot accurately price the care Medicare beneficiaries actually receive. Correcting this is not a workforce advocacy issue. It is a data accuracy issue for the Medicare program itself.
>
> Thank you for opening this question to public input. I would welcome the opportunity for CMS to engage the nursing and nursing informatics communities directly as this work continues.
>
> Respectfully,
>
> [NAME, CREDENTIALS]
> [ROLE, CITY, STATE]

## Adaptation Notes by Audience

**Bedside nurses.** Cut sections 3 and 4 if needed. The most valuable thing you can submit is section 2: one concrete, specific story of clinical judgment you exercised that appeared nowhere on a bill. Two paragraphs from lived experience beats five pages of policy argument.

**Nursing informatics professionals.** Expand section 3. If your institution has built or piloted structured nursing documentation with time capture or decision capture, describe it and offer the data pattern (never patient data) as proof of concept.

**Nurse leaders and CNOs.** Add institutional scale to section 4: number of RNs, patient volume, and what invisible nursing work means at your organization's size. If commenting on behalf of your organization rather than as an individual, get institutional sign-off first.

**Educators and researchers.** Cite the staffing-outcomes literature you know best in section 4. CMS comments may include references.
